A **demand response curve** is a concept from economics and decision theory that describes **how the quantity demanded of a good, service, or resource changes in response to changes in another variable**, most commonly **price**.
It is a graphical representation of **consumer behavior**.

### Demand Response Curve
![350](images/Pasted%20image%2020260718060743.png)
Price is always x axis, demand is the y axis.

![500](images/Pasted%20image%2020260718073559)
#### Latent Demand
Increase in demand as price goes down from $P^*$ to $P_1$.  (The orange region)
#### Consumer Surplus
Decrease in demand as the price goes up from $P^*$ to $P_2$. (Blue region)
Gap between what people are willing to pay and what they actually pay.

> $P^*$ is the optimum price with certain demand and price which can be based on maximizing the profit or revenue. Whatever is the goal.

If we lower the price we can tap in the market that is in latent demand. If we increase the prices we could tap into the consumer surplus (the max they are willing to pay). Getting more people to buy by lowering prices (tapping into latent demand) doesn't always maximize the profit. That depends on both the prices and demand.

To find out how much the demand changes by changing the price we have two measures. (Quantification of demand response)
